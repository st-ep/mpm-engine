# Single-pinch force-control pilot

This experiment tests whether the existing A and B specimens respond
differently to the same force command, without imposing a final finger gap.
It is separate from the four-pinch X study and does not replace the paper
figure. It does not yet test identification-based planning.

The runner is `experiments/robotics/x_pinch_force_pilot.py`. The diagnostic
renderer is `experiments/robotics/x_pinch_force_report.py`. The main archive
is `out/x_pinch_force_pilot_20260909/`; earlier controller qualification
is preserved in `out/x_pinch_force_qualification_20260909/`.

## Fixed physical setup

The specimen, gravity, constitutive laws, and contacts come from the current
rounded-X experiment. Both specimens are 60 by 60 by 25 mm, with 90 mL
reference volume and density 1000 kg/m³. Both have Hencky elasticity with
E = 80 kPa and nu = 0.30. The von Mises yield thresholds are 1 kPa for A
and 10 kPa for B. These are the existing rate-independent material models.
The true laws are copied from the cross-execution archive; no identification
or material retuning takes place in this pilot.

The two vertical cylinders are 28 mm in diameter and 45 mm tall. Their
bottoms are 0.5 mm above the support. The single pinch closes along y,
at the center of the specimen. Tool and support friction coefficients
remain 0.3 and 0.5. The signed-distance field uses 1 mm cells and a 0.2 mm
contact band. The baseline MPM grid is 64³ in a 160 mm domain, with a
50 microsecond time step and 4 ms controller period.

## Action and feedback

1. Lower the open fingers, then approach from 72 to 64 mm physical opening
   at 10 mm/s per finger. Wait 0.2 s before the pulse. This preparation is
   the same for every run, including the zero-force control.
2. Apply a two-second force command: 0.4 s raised-cosine rise, 1.2 s constant
   plateau, and 0.4 s raised-cosine fall. Only its amplitude varies.
3. Open to 72 mm at 50 mm/s per finger, lift at 100 mm/s, and observe the
   material one second after full withdrawal. This delay does not assert
   that the material is exactly at equilibrium.

Force is measured separately at each collider from its accumulated MPM
grid reaction impulse over the controller period. The outward y projection
is positive compression. Feedback is the mean of these two signed forces,
filtered with a 12 ms exponential time constant. Their vector sum is not
used because opposing finger forces cancel at the wrist.

The PI controller maps force error to inward speed per finger:

```
error = requested_force - filtered_measured_force
integral_velocity += Ki * error * tick
inward_velocity = Kp * error + integral_velocity
```

The final common gains are Kp = 0.050 m/(s N) and Ki = 0.040 m/(s² N).
Speed is bounded by 50 mm/s, acceleration by 0.5 m/s², and physical
opening by 4 to 72 mm. Conditional integration prevents the integral term
from accumulating further into an active saturation. The full code defines
the discrete update and records all limit activations. The minimum opening
is a common travel guard, not a shaping target. Outcomes that activate it
must not be interpreted as successful force tracking.

Only the cylinder contacts enter the material simulation. Their kinematic
motion is generated by the feedback controller. Neither Franka actuator
dynamics nor physical force-sensor performance is simulated.

## Qualification and measurements

The first controller used Kp = 0.020 and Ki = 0.080. At 2 N per finger,
material A reached the 4 mm travel guard while B tracked the commanded
load. That pair is unsuitable as evidence of equal applied force. At 1 N,
neither material reached a limit, but A overshot the plateau appreciably.
The gain change above was made to improve common force tracking, before
freezing the main amplitude sweep. Both controller versions and all eight
qualification outcomes are retained. Qualification is controller development,
not an independent confirmation of the final protocol.

The main baseline sweep requests 0, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, and 2 N per finger.
Zero force measures deformation from gravity and the common preparation.
Higher forces characterize the boundary of feasible force regulation and
are retained even when they fail to track. Tracking error, individual jaw
forces, speed/acceleration/travel saturation, and finger gap are recorded
at every controller tick. Particle states are recorded every 0.1 s during
the pulse, at minimum separation, and after release.

The plateau mean and RMSE use pulse times 0.6 < t <= 1.6 s, excluding the
first 0.2 s of the constant plateau. Whole-pulse mean absolute tracking
error and the A/B measured-force discrepancy include the rise and fall.

The main deformation measure is the volume-weighted RMS displacement of
material particles from their initial positions to the released state.
Particle indices are preserved. This is displacement, not target-shape
error, and includes the effects of gravity and preparation. The zero-force
control is reported separately, without subtracting it from other results.

An auxiliary waist measure is the full y extent of the reconstructed
released surface at the center plane x = 80 mm. Reconstruction uses the existing
reference-volume method with 1.25 mm voxels, Gaussian sigma 1.3 voxels,
and density isovalue 0.5. The audit also uses 1.0 and 1.5 mm voxels.
Raw particle widths in a 5 mm central strip, the 1st-to-99th percentile
width, residual speed, and floor penetration
provide additional checks. No geometry registration or rescaling is used.

Numerical refinements retain the same physical force pulse and feedback
settings. The feedback naturally generates different trajectories when
the numerical solution changes. This differs from replaying a frozen
position trajectory.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, use the project virtual environment. Use a fresh
`--out` directory for a new run, passing it to every command below.

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_pilot prepare
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_pilot sweep --material A
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_pilot sweep --material B
```

The two material sweeps can run concurrently because their outputs are
disjoint. Baseline and half-time-step runs use GPU 0. Finer-grid runs use
GPU 1, concurrently with another job. No other job was stopped. There is
no timing comparison. The refined subset is 0, 0.75, 1, and 1.25 N for
both materials; controller period and pulse duration stay fixed.

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_pilot sweep --material A --setting half_dt --amplitudes 0.75 1 1.25 0
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_pilot sweep --material B --setting half_dt --amplitudes 0.75 1 1.25 0
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_pilot sweep --material A --setting grid80 --device cuda:1 --amplitudes 1 0.75 1.25 0
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_pilot sweep --material B --setting grid80 --device cuda:1 --amplitudes 1 0.75 1.25 0
```

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_pilot verify
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_report audit
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_pinch_force_report report --selected-peak 1.25
```

`protocol.json`, `models.json`, per-run configurations, phase records,
raw NPZ data, package versions, and source snapshots define the runs.
Hash checks reject changes to the frozen physics or inputs. Each controller
revision requires its own source snapshot; running the final source against
the earlier qualification archive intentionally fails the source check.
The manuscript, its PDF and figure, and unrelated tracked edits are checked
against their pre-pilot versions.

## Results and interpretation

All 28 final rollouts completed: twelve baseline runs and eight runs for
each numerical refinement. Eight earlier controller-qualification runs
remain separately archived. These are fixed-seed simulation cases,
not repeated hardware trials or a statistical sample of materials.

At the baseline resolution, the main results are:

| Requested peak per finger (N) | A measured plateau (N) | B measured plateau (N) | A RMS displacement (mm) | B RMS displacement (mm) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.236 | 0.183 |
| 0.5 | 0.525 | 0.519 | 0.248 | 0.183 |
| 0.75 | 0.789 | 0.770 | 0.405 | 0.183 |
| 1 | 1.036 | 1.022 | 1.373 | 0.183 |
| 1.25 | 1.141 | 1.273 | 8.511 | 0.183 |
| 2 | 0.694 | 2.027 | 11.947 | 0.180 |

The 2 N run for A reaches the common 4 mm travel guard and fails to
regulate the load. It is shown as a failed force-tracking case, not a
successful equal-force experiment. It is not used as the selected example.

The selected 1.25 N command produces a pronounced pinch after release in A
while B returns close to its initial shape. Neither specimen reaches the
speed or travel limit at this amplitude in any numerical setting. Their
minimum baseline openings are 13.725056 and 58.265635 mm, respectively.
The pulse amplitude and duration are the same, but the measured force
histories are not identical. A's baseline plateau RMSE is 0.112642 N;
B's is 0.023336 N. The RMS A/B force difference across the whole pulse
is 0.171327 N. Thus the supported description is **same force command
and controller**, not exactly identical applied force.

The released-state displacement contrast persists under both refinements:

| Setting | A RMS displacement (mm) | B RMS displacement (mm) | A minimum gap (mm) | B minimum gap (mm) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 64³, 50 microseconds | 8.510768 | 0.182808 | 13.725056 | 58.265635 |
| 64³, 25 microseconds | 8.214425 | 0.181679 | 15.094627 | 58.283650 |
| 80³, 50 microseconds | 7.637856 | 0.151673 | 18.754660 | 58.351202 |

The force and shape contrast is robust in these checks, while A's precise
trajectory and contour retain numerical sensitivity. This is not a
convergence proof. The 1 N case provides a reference with closer force
tracking and a smaller deformation contrast.

Surface-waist estimates are sensitive to smoothing because the deformed
specimen has thin regions near its indentations. At baseline, A's 1.25 N
waist estimates are 40.618, 37.272, and 27.269 mm for 1.0, 1.25, and 1.5 mm
reconstruction voxels. The quantitative figure labels therefore use raw
particle RMS displacement. The complete raw particle view is also saved.
The untrimmed central-strip particle widths are 44.518 and 59.261 mm for
A and B at baseline, and 46.601 and 59.390 mm on the finer grid.

All final runs have finite recorded states, zero final inverted particles,
fixed reference volume, and one connected reconstructed surface. The
maximum sampled height is 33.256 mm above the support, below the 45.5 mm
finger working height. The maximum sampled floor penetration is 1.251 mm;
particles are not clipped from the reported displacement. Maximum final
RMS speed is 0.029724 mm/s. Force after full withdrawal is zero in every run.

The result supports continuing with force-profile planning for the four
pinches. It establishes material-dependent response under a common
controller. It does not yet show that identified models plan an accurate X,
that exchanged plans fail, or that the physical Franka gripper reproduces
these low force profiles. The manuscript and its active figure are unchanged.
