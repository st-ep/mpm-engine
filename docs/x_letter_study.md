# Rounded-X shaping with identified materials A and B

The active paper now compares the two identified plans on both materials.
That cross-execution extension is documented in `docs/x_letter_cross.md`
and generated by `experiments/robotics/x_letter_cross_report.py`. This file
retains the original planning protocol and nominal-comparison results.

This study uses the revised X approved after the material A feasibility work.
The target is fixed before all searches in this comparison. It replaces the
earlier notched-square, two-pinch shaping experiment when adopted in the paper.
The separate press calibration remains the source of the identified models.

## Sources and scope

- `experiments/robotics/x_letter_control.py`: material-parameterized MPM
  execution with four independent physical finger openings, in y, x, y, x order.
- `experiments/robotics/x_letter_study.py`: frozen inputs, equal-budget
  optimization, true-material execution, numerical repeats, and verification.
- `experiments/robotics/x_letter_study_report.py`: geometry audit and paper
  figure, with the analytic target read from this study's frozen `target.vtp`.
- `experiments/robotics/x_shaping_franka.py`: proposed adapters, Panda hand
  visualization, and sampled position/velocity checks for the recorded motions.
- `out/x_letter_study_20260909/`: authoritative protocol, source snapshots,
  inputs, raw rollouts, evaluation history, selected controls, and figure data.
- `out/x_shaping_identification_20260909/`: source of the reused press data
  and parameter estimates. Copies and hashes are in the new study's `inputs/`.
- `out/x_letter_target_redesign_20260909/`: target-design provenance.

The older `x_letter_pilot.py` and `x_letter_refine.py` executors are A-only
feasibility studies using true parameters and earlier targets. They are not
the source of the new identified-model comparisons. The old `x_shaping_study.py`
and its `hardware_timing/` replays describe a different two-pinch target.
Their metrics must not be reassigned to this experiment.

Both materials have Hencky elasticity and von Mises plasticity, E = 80 kPa,
nu = 0.30, and density = 1000 kg/m³. Yield thresholds are 1 kPa for A and
10 kPa for B; the shared nominal threshold is 5.5 kPa. Identified parameters
come from the existing, separate supplied-state press experiment. The fit does
not use shaping outcomes. This is a known-family simulation study, not
constitutive-family selection or identification from hardware images.

## Fixed task and control

The target is a rounded capital X, 64 × 80 mm in plan and 24.946587 mm high.
Its 90 mL volume matches the original 60 × 60 × 25 mm specimen. The frozen
target mesh has SHA-256
`6e3e29fb5d3a50cb3466d7ec30d3ec07cca6a339fc09f13510931c8bd7710784`.
The `target_width` and `target_notch_radius` fields inherited in the protocol's
shared scene dictionary are unused legacy settings. Scoring and outcome
rendering read the frozen `target.vtp`; they do not call the old target generator.

The target and common initialization were informed by previous true-A
feasibility trials. The previous posthoc target-fit scores are not treated as
new control results. Every model in this study plans against the same target,
using the same starting openings [20, 31, 35, 20] mm, a simplex with each
coordinate reduced by 3 mm in turn, bounds [16, 54] mm, and 32 bounded
Nelder–Mead objective evaluations. Selection uses the lowest observed objective;
the fixed budget does not establish global or local convergence. One nominal
plan is executed in both true materials. Identified-A and identified-B plans
are each executed in their corresponding true material. This comparison does
not include a new true-parameter optimization reference.

The four controlled parameters are final cylinder-surface openings. Positions
are centered on the specimen, with orientations 90°, 0°, 90°, 0°. The fingers
have diameter 28 mm and height 45 mm. Each pinch starts at a 72 mm opening,
approaches vertically, closes symmetrically at up to 50 mm/s per finger,
reopens, and withdraws. Vertical motion is at most 100 mm/s. Raised quarter
turns take 1 s in both planning and execution. The final observation is 1 s
after complete withdrawal. There is no force limit, work budget, shape
feedback, or online replanning.

The baseline MPM grid is 64³ with a 50 microsecond timestep. Each of the four
selected true-material executions is repeated at 25 microseconds and,
separately, on an 80³ grid. Physical geometry, particle reference volume,
target, and selected commands remain fixed. Particle placement uses seed 0.
Contact is separable Coulomb friction: 0.3 at the cylinders and 0.5 at the floor.

The objective is symmetric, area-weighted mean surface distance in mm, using
triangle-centroid quadrature and closest-triangle distances. Surfaces are
reconstructed from reference particle volumes on 1.25 mm voxels, with Gaussian
sigma 1.625 mm and isovalue 0.5. No alignment, rescaling, particle clipping,
or reconstruction-bias subtraction is applied. Footprint overlap is a
supplementary orthographic silhouette IoU at common camera and scale.

Baseline means are 1.319186 mm for the nominal plan and 1.179156 mm for
the identified plans. Per-material reductions are 14.31% for A and 7.44%
for B. The reductions on the finer grid are 20.69% and 1.00%, respectively.
B's small finer-grid advantage should not be presented as a large or
resolution-independent benefit.

The objective is 3D surface distance, not silhouette overlap. At baseline,
A has top-view IoU 89.14% with the identified plan and 91.57% with the
nominal plan; B has 83.62% and 80.31%, respectively. Thus the lower 3D
error does not establish improvement in every outline region or metric.
On the finer grid the corresponding IoUs are 92.13% versus 91.48% for A,
and 87.76% versus 84.02% for B. These supplementary IoUs use the recorded
650 × 700 orthographic masks and are not optimization objectives.

### Completed verification

- All 96 planning evaluations are distinct raw rollouts. Together with
  12 true-material executions, 108 rollouts pass source, input, objective,
  selected-minimum, phase-endpoint, gap, timing, and physical-volume checks.
- All retained states are finite and have zero recorded inversions.
  The nominal A/B command times and tool centers are identical. Raised
  rotations have zero recorded contact force.
- All 12 selected surfaces have one connected component. Common top and
  oblique views contain each entire outcome without cropping. The
  maximum pressed height is 34.119962 mm, below the 45.5 mm working height.
- The identified-versus-nominal 3D ordering holds at 1.0, 1.25, and 1.5 mm
  reconstruction voxels in all three numerical settings, although the
  finer-grid B difference is only 0.00189 mm at 1.5 mm voxels. Refining
  target-surface quadrature changes a score by at most 0.004726 mm.
- Maximum particle floor penetration is 1.628025 mm. At most 2.753906%
  of particles lie below the plane; the maximum volume-weighted mean
  penetration is 0.015612 mm. These raw particles are retained in scoring.
- All four sampled Panda trajectories pass arm position/speed and hand
  travel checks. Maximum position residual is 0.000481 mm; peak joint
  speed is 1.549893 rad/s. Stock hand travel spans 24–80 mm. This is a
  kinematic check, not hardware, collision, acceleration, or torque validation.
- Six existing geometry/distance tests pass. Ruff passes for the new
  experiment and reporting modules. VTK emits existing NumPy deprecation
  warnings in the tests; headless rendering uses the available fallback.

`summary.json`, `additional_audit.json`, `calibration_recheck.json`,
`franka/audit.json`, and `figure_provenance.json` retain the detailed
measurements and source mapping. `report_source/` freezes the figure
implementation. The earlier calibration force errors were independently
recomputed from their copied raw force arrays and match the manuscript.

## Reproduction

Use the recorded package environment and run from the repository root. GPU 0
was used for this experiment; GPU 1 belonged to separate work.

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_letter_study prepare
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_letter_study complete
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_letter_study_report audit
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_shaping_franka --out out/x_letter_study_20260909 --check-speeds
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.x_letter_study_report report --render-out out/x_letter_nominal_rerender
```

`prepare` requires a fresh directory. To repeat from scratch, pass the same
fresh `--out` path to all study commands and the robot audit. To regenerate
from saved results, skip `prepare`; `verify`, `audit`, and `report` use the
archived raw data. Searches can resume from exact matching cached rollouts;
their full 32-entry objective histories and selected minima are preserved.
Source and input hashes prevent mixing changed code or target data with an
existing study. Small GPU reduction differences can affect a fresh search;
the saved outputs reproduce the reported metrics and figure.

The rendering command above reproduces the historical nominal comparison
without replacing the active paper figure. Use `docs/x_letter_cross.md`
for the current figure and PDF rebuild.

The recorded run used `out/x_letter_study_20260909/run_parallel.py` to run the
three independent model searches concurrently on GPU 0, then execute their
selected commands sequentially. An initial B-only search was interrupted and
resumed from its exact cached rollouts. No targets, material properties,
initializations, bounds, or budgets were changed. The wrapper and individual
logs are retained; no runtime comparison is claimed from these executions.

The Panda hand is a kinematic illustration with proposed adapters. Only the
cylinders and support contact the MPM specimen. Sampled position/velocity
checks do not validate acceleration, jerk, collisions, forces, or hardware
execution. Grid contact permits subcell particle penetration of the floor;
the additional audit records this and tests reconstruction sensitivity.
Numerical repeats are sensitivity checks, not a convergence proof.

The unrelated Table II values and timing scope remain unchanged.
