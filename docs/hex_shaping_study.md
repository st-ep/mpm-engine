# Identification and hexagonal shaping study

This is the archived position-control study. The active manuscript now uses
[surface-objective work control](hex_shaping_surface.md); the identification
and held-out press inputs below are still reused unchanged.

This study replaced the rectangular shaping example in the manuscript's
four-panel identification/shaping figure. It uses the hexagonal target and
revised B from the earlier feasibility experiment, with new identification,
held-out press validation, and a nominal-versus-identified planning comparison.
The previous studies and their raw outputs remain archived unchanged.

## Materials and observations

The initial particles and visible appearance are identical: a nominal
120 × 80 × 60 mm block, density 1000 kg/m³, Poisson's ratio 0.30, and the
same von Mises model with Hencky elasticity. A has E = 80 kPa and yield
threshold 1 kPa. B has E = 240 kPa and yield threshold 10 kPa. B's increased
stiffness was selected during exploratory feasibility tests to retain clearer
facets after release; it is not a measured hardware property.

Each material receives one 14 mm press at approximately 80 mm/s. The
identifier receives noise-free simulated particle positions, velocities,
elastic deformation gradients, mass, and reference volume. It fits the
elastic moduli from the time-weak momentum balance and estimates yield from
the elastic-strain plateau. Stress labels and measured reaction forces do
not enter identification. The simulation supplies the elastic states;
this does not establish recovery from positions or visual appearance alone.
Both specimens use the same known constitutive family, without family selection.

The held-out press uses a 24 mm stroke at 120 mm/s. Force errors are relative
L2 discrepancies over its entire recorded loading trace. The shared nominal
model remains the previous study's E = 80 kPa, nu = 0.30, yield = 5.5 kPa.
It has A's true elastic parameters but does not have B's revised stiffness.
Its parameters were not tuned to the hexagonal outcomes.

## Target, controls, and comparison

Both specimens aim for the same analytic regular hexagonal prism: 90 mm
across flats, 81.6666950594 mm high, volume 0.0005728760037726488 m³. This
equals the baseline initial particle volume. The target is specified
geometrically rather than generated by a true-material action. Its center
is fixed at x = y = 150 mm, with its bottom at the floor; no rigid alignment
or rescaling is performed when scoring an outcome.

The control variables are three final jaw gaps. Two flat jaws squeeze along
0°, 60°, and 120°, then repeat the three-direction sequence once, reusing
the gaps. Every pair starts at a 160 mm opening and each jaw closes at
approximately 100 mm/s. Motion duration is quantized to the 0.8 ms control
period, with a small velocity adjustment to reach the requested gap.
The pair is deactivated after closing, followed by 0.30 s of free evolution.
The contact model idealizes insertion, withdrawal, and changes of orientation;
it does not plan robot joint trajectories. This is open-loop kinematic
control, without force feedback or online replanning.

Every planner uses bounded Nelder-Mead with gaps in [40, 115] mm, the same
initial gaps (80, 85, 87) mm, the same simplex obtained by subtracting 10 mm
from each coordinate in turn, and a budget of 32 objective evaluations.
There are five searches: the shared nominal law, the two identified laws,
and the two true-parameter references. The nominal search is shared because
the initial geometry and target are identical. Selected gaps are stored to
1e-6 mm and are not rounded to whole millimetres in this comparison.

The objective evaluates the shape 0.30 s after final release. It is the mean
of the two directed mean nearest-neighbor distances between full particle
clouds, in millimetres, without squaring distances. Each selected plan is
then independently executed using the corresponding specimen's true law.
The paper reports shape error 1.0 s after release. Search budgets are finite;
the selected actions are the best found, without claiming global optimality.
The true-parameter result is a planning reference with the same limited
search budget, not an exact achievable optimum.

There is one common target and two material-target tasks. Target geometry,
action sequence, and revised B follow exploratory feasibility work. These
two tasks do not establish general performance over arbitrary targets or
materials. The previous six-task rectangular aggregate cannot be transferred
to the new figure.

The independent executions give the following full-cloud errors 1.0 s after
release:

| Material | Nominal (mm) | Identified (mm) | True-parameter reference (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 1.67334642 | 1.58577201 | 1.58611302 |
| B | 1.71489676 | 1.54613639 | 1.54530108 |
| Mean | 1.69412159 | 1.56595420 | 1.56570705 |

Identification improves both baseline tasks, but the advantage over this
nominal model is modest. The identified plans are close to the true-parameter
references. The finite action sequence and search budget, physical response,
and discrete point sampling all contribute to the remaining discrepancy
against the analytic target. These errors are not directly comparable with
the old rectangular task's errors against simulated reachable targets.

## Numerical checks and artifacts

The baseline is the existing 48³ MPM grid in a 0.30 m domain, 0.1 ms
timestep, two particles per cell direction, and seed 0 (18,772 particles).
The floor and rotated box-SDF jaws use sticky contact. Jaw half-extents are
12.5 / 95 / 80 mm, SDF spacing is 2 mm, and the contact band is 0.2 mm.
Gap placement accounts for that band.

All six selected executions are repeated with a half timestep and on a
64³ grid without retuning their actions. The finer grid changes initial
particle volume to 0.0005804386289476327 m³ and refines target sampling, while
retaining the same analytic target dimensions. These are sensitivity checks,
not a convergence estimate. Both 0.30 s and 1.0 s released states, residual
velocities, and every compressed/intermediate released stage are retained.

| Numerical setting | A nominal (mm) | A identified (mm) | B nominal (mm) | B identified (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 48³, 0.05 ms | 1.68786479 | 1.58077741 | 1.73261942 | 1.54450426 |
| 64³, 0.1 ms | 1.50380819 | 1.41798192 | 1.28771809 | 1.17640943 |

Both checks preserve the improvement over the nominal plans. All executions
remain finite with zero inverted particles. Independent replays of the
selected true-model plans differ from their searched trajectories by
0.00030778 mm for A and 0.00043863 mm for B at the 0.30 s objective time.
The verification stage recomputed all 160 search objectives, all six reported
execution errors, both force comparisons, and the numerical errors from raw
data. Twelve focused tests and Ruff passed.

`out/hex_shaping_study_20260908/` contains the frozen protocol, complete
simulation and identification source snapshots, package versions, GPU
information, probes, fitted laws, validation traces, all search rollouts,
selected plans, independent executions, numerical checks, and checksums.
`shaping_results.json` contains individual errors and selected gaps;
`summary.json` contains force errors, mean shaping errors, numerical checks,
and independent true-model replay discrepancies.

To avoid repeating identical calculations, the original run imported 32 A
and 24 B true-parameter search rollouts from the preceding feasibility study.
Material law, initial simplex, target samples, discretization, and hashes of
all archived simulation sources were checked first. Imports and their hashes
are recorded in `reused_true_rollouts.json`. The searches still recompute
their objectives and select from 32 evaluations per model. A fresh reproduction
does not need these caches and computes the same cases itself. CUDA reductions
can cause small floating-point differences and occasionally a different
optimization path.

The four-panel renderer is `experiments/robotics/hex_shaping_figure.py`.
It preserves the established layout and press illustration. Action images
use the saved rotated SDF jaw poses; the mounting hardware in the press
images and closing arrows are visual annotations. Outcomes use a density
surface at 2.5 mm voxel spacing, Gaussian sigma 1.3 voxels, and isovalue 0.5.
The analytic target uses an exact flat-shaded mesh. Both materials and all
outcomes share a camera and scale. Dashed target silhouettes use a 0.55 pt
black vector stroke without a white halo. Numerical errors use raw particle
clouds, independently of rendering. Renderer sources and figure hashes are
saved in `figure_source/` and `figure_provenance.json`.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, using a fresh output directory and the existing
CUDA-capable `.venv`:

```bash
HEX_STUDY_OUT=out/hex_shaping_study_reproduction
HEX_STUDY_DEVICE=cuda:0
.venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.hex_shaping_study prepare --out "$HEX_STUDY_OUT" --device "$HEX_STUDY_DEVICE"
for HEX_STUDY_MODEL in nominal identified_A identified_B true_A true_B; do
  .venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.hex_shaping_study plan --out "$HEX_STUDY_OUT" --device "$HEX_STUDY_DEVICE" --model "$HEX_STUDY_MODEL"
done
.venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.hex_shaping_study evaluate --out "$HEX_STUDY_OUT" --device "$HEX_STUDY_DEVICE"
.venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.hex_shaping_study verify --out "$HEX_STUDY_OUT"
.venv/bin/python -m experiments.robotics.hex_shaping_figure --out "$HEX_STUDY_OUT" --dest "$HEX_STUDY_OUT/identification_plastic_shaping"
.venv/bin/pytest -q tests/test_hex_shaping_control.py tests/test_hex_shaping_pilot.py tests/test_plastic_shaping_study.py
```

Independent `plan` commands may run concurrently. Source/settings checks
reject resuming after a scientific code change. Keep reproduction plots in
the new output directory; updating the manuscript figure uses an explicit
`--dest paper/icra2027/figs/identification_plastic_shaping`, followed by
`./paper/icra2027/build.sh` and page inspection.
